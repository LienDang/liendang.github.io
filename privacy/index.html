Privacy notice

Last updated 4 November 2026

This is Lien Dang's personal portfolio. It has no accounts, sign-ups or ads, and it sets no cookies. This page explains what leaves your browser when you visit, including the visitor statistics that show me how the site is used, and how to opt out of them.

Visitor statistics

Visitor statistics aren't active in this browser right now, so nothing is sent to Umami.

The site uses Umami Cloud, a privacy-focused analytics service, to count visits and see how the portfolio and its Lab are used, so I can improve them. Umami doesn't set cookies or store anything in your browser, and it says it doesn't track visitors across other websites. I use the results only as statistics about this site: I don't try to identify individual visitors, and nothing is used for advertising.

Umami records:

  • Each page you open: its address (keeping any utm_ campaign tags but no other query parameters), its title, and the site you came from, without the query part of that site's address.
  • Your browser and device: browser, operating system, device type, screen size and language, and an approximate location (country, region and city) that Umami works out from your IP address.
  • How the page performs: how fast it loads and responds, and any script errors, with web addresses shortened and anything that looks like an email address or password removed.
  • What you do on the page: which sections you reach and how far down you scroll; how long the page is on screen (after 30 seconds, 1, 3 and 10 minutes); which links you click, including the email link, recorded without the query part of their address (and, for UniProt, without the protein's ID); that you copied some text, and from which section, but never what you copied; printing; and opening the Python example.
  • What you do in the Lab: looking up a food (whether you tapped or typed it, and whether proteins were found; a typed food is counted once you stop typing), picking a protein (its public UniProt ID, but only for a food the Lab already knows), pressing Translate, mutating a DNA base and what kind of mutation it was, shuffling codons, undoing mutations, and clicking the codon wheel or the amino acid classes. A lookup names the food only if it's one the Lab already knows, like milk or rice. Anything else you type is recorded as "other", so your own words are never sent to Umami.

Umami tells repeat visits apart without cookies, using details your browser sends with each request. It says it doesn't store visitor IP addresses in the analytics records, though it and its infrastructure providers may keep them in operational and security logs. Umami processes the statistics on my behalf under its data processing agreement, keeps them for 6 months on the plan this site uses, and may process them outside your country, including in the United States. See Umami's privacy policy and security page.

Because the statistics are limited to measuring and improving this site, they're collected without asking first, based on my legitimate interest in understanding how the portfolio is used. You can object at any time by opting out below.

Opting out

Opting out stops this browser's visits being counted, straight away and in every open tab. The choice is saved in this browser's local storage as umami.disabled, the opt-out flag Umami itself checks. That's the only thing the site stores in your browser, and only if you opt out. Clearing this site's data in your browser undoes it.

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, the site treats it as opting out and counts nothing. Content blockers that block Umami have the same effect.

Opting out can't remove statistics already collected. If you'd like those removed, email me and I'll do what Umami's tools allow.

Services the site always uses

These apply whether or not your visits are counted.

  • Hosting. GitHub Pages serves the site, so GitHub receives the usual request details, such as your IP address, whenever your browser loads a page. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Fonts. Each page loads the Inter typeface from Google Fonts, so your browser also contacts Google's servers, which receive your IP address and browser details. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.
  • Lab lookups. The Lab searches UniProt, the public protein database, straight from your browser: once for milk when the homepage opens, then whenever you pick a food or pause while typing one. UniProt receives each search, which can include what you typed, and your IP address. See UniProt's privacy notice.
